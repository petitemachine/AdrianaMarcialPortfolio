Research-driven UX case study

Dental Anxiety App

An exploratory research project translating patient concerns and a practising dentist’s perspective into an app concept for clearer information, communication and patient control.

Research & findingsDesign decisionsDeliverables & next steps

Research to design

Research scope: exploratory convenience sample and one practising dentist interview. The completed-response count and original survey data have not been verified, so no participant totals, percentages or statistical claims are presented here.

01

Project & research question

For my user-centred design module at DCU, I explored how a mobile experience could support people with low-to-mild dental anxiety. The question was: how might clearer information and a simple way to communicate discomfort help patients feel more prepared and in control? My role covered research, synthesis and UX design; the output is a design concept.

02

Discover · exploratory survey

The online questionnaire explored experiences, concerns and information needs around dental appointments. An exploratory survey recruited through a personal network using convenience sampling. Findings are directional and cannot be generalised to the wider patient population. Recruitment through personal contacts may introduce selection and response bias.

03

Discover · expert interview

I interviewed a practising dentist to bring a clinical perspective to patient anxiety, communication and the appointment experience. This complemented the patient survey with a professional viewpoint. One interview provides context for design exploration; it does not establish clinical consensus or validate the proposed app.

04

Define · research findings

The existing project synthesis identifies pain and needles, uncertainty before a visit, and difficulty communicating during treatment as concerns to explore. These themes helped frame a need for preparation and a sense of control. I developed the persona John around a past painful experience to focus the design. These are exploratory themes, not a verified ranking of anxiety factors.

05

Develop · from insight to design

Uncertainty before a visit → preparation content: explain what to expect in clear, manageable steps.
Difficulty speaking during treatment → Comfort Scale: propose a simple way to signal discomfort or request a pause.
Support across the appointment journey → treatment-plan view: bring preparation, in-appointment communication and follow-up information into one concept.

06

Deliver · concept & deliverables

The project produced an app concept and a Comfort Scale interaction, originally explored in Adobe XD. The visual refinement shown above presents the before, during and after journey. It demonstrates how research can guide content and interaction decisions. No validated reduction in anxiety, clinical benefit or measured product impact is reported.

07

Next steps · validation & accessibility

The original Comfort Scale used traffic-light colours and hearts. A next iteration should pair colour with explicit text such as “Continue”, “Pause” and “Stop”, then test comprehension, contrast, touch targets and assistive-technology support. Broader patient recruitment and usability sessions with patients and dental professionals would help assess whether using a phone during treatment is practical, and whether the dentist notices and correctly interprets the signal.

08

Reflection · content & web practice

This project strengthened my ability to turn exploratory research into clear information and purposeful design decisions. The same approach informs my content and web work: understand the audience, prioritise what they need, make the next action clear and validate the experience before claiming success.
